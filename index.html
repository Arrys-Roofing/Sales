How to work a route

  1. Go in stop order. Open the route, then use the List tab for the notes on each home.
  2. Tier A and B get the full conversation. Tier C homes likely don't need a roof right now, so stop only if the timing works. A quick hello can still turn up a leak or a solar problem.
  3. Look before knocking. Check that panels are on the roof and note the roof's condition from the street.
  4. No answer? Come back once at a different time of day, then move on. Weekdays 4–7 pm and Saturday 10–2 are the likeliest times to find owners home.
  5. Log every door with the date and outcome. The Excel file has columns for this.

The pitch

The goal at the door is a booked inspection, not a roof sale. Keep it to two minutes. Each home's card in the List tab has an Angle line with the best opening for that house.

1. Open

“Hi, I'm Jamie with Arry's Roofing, a local roofer. I'm stopping by homes in the neighborhood that have solar panels from installers that have since closed. When the installer goes out of business, there's usually nobody left looking after the roof around those panels. We're offering a free check of the roof and the panel mounts. Do you have a minute?”

2. Ask, then listen

  • “Who's been looking after the system since [installer] closed?”
  • “Any water stains on the ceilings, or drips in the attic, especially under the panels?”
  • “Do you know how old the roof is under the panels?”
  • “Has your insurance company asked about the roof's age at renewal?”
  • “Are you planning to stay in the home for a while?”

3. Why it matters to them

  • Every array means dozens of holes in the roof. Each mount is sealed and flashed, and Florida sun and storms wear that seal down over time.
  • The installer usually stood behind those roof penetrations. With the installer gone, a leak around a mount may now be the homeowner's problem.
  • A new roof under solar means taking the panels off and putting them back. That goes better when it's planned than after a leak.
  • Small problems found early, like a cracked boot, loose flashing or a bad seal, cost far less than water damage to decking and ceilings.

4. Close

“The check takes about 30 to 45 minutes and there's no cost. You'll get photos of what we find and an honest answer on how much life the roof has left. I have [day] or [day]. Which works better?”

Get a name and phone number, and confirm everyone on the title can be there. Cards flagged “Multiple owners,” “Life estate” or “Trust” may need more than one signer.

6. After every door

  • Leave a card or door hanger, even at a no-answer, with a short handwritten note: “Stopped by about the roof under your solar panels.”
  • Log the date and outcome in the Excel file.
  • Text booked homeowners a reminder the day before.

Draft scripts. Replace the bracketed parts, confirm Arry's offers a free inspection, and add a line about Arry's (years in business, license) once you have it.

Jamie can say

  • Name the installer as closed only when its badge says Confirmed.
  • Installer workmanship coverage is usually the part at risk when an installer fails.
  • Panel and inverter manufacturer warranties usually continue, so they're worth checking.
  • A new roof under an array means taking the panels off and putting them back.

Jamie should not say

  • Anything about whether a solar loan or lease can be cancelled, or what a contract means.
  • That an insurance claim could pay for the roof. Florida law restricts roofers here.
  • “Your warranty is void.” Jamie hasn't seen their paperwork.
  • Details from this page, like home value, sale price or who is on the title.

Before the first door

  • Solicitor rules. County records put all 98 homes in unincorporated Hillsborough County, despite the Tampa addresses. Check the county's door-to-door rules, which I did not confirm, and HOA restrictions. Honor every no-soliciting sign.
  • Contracts signed at the home. Have counsel confirm the required cancellation notice and roofing contract language, including Fla. Stat. 489.147, parts of which have been challenged in court.
  • Detach and reset. Line up a licensed solar partner and a price before Jamie discusses it.

      How the tiers work

      ATop priority. Homestead, owner on title since before the solar went in, and no re-roof permit on file for a house built before 2017.
      BNext priority. Roof permit is 10–11 years old, or the owner bought after the solar went on, or no homestead on record.
      CIf time allows. Roof under 10 years, newer house, or already re-roofed or detached since the solar went on.

      Limits of the roof data: county permit records get thin before about 2015, and unpermitted work won't appear. “No re-roof permit” means the roof may be old. It does not prove it. Permit dollar figures are what was filed with the county and may not match the signed contract.
