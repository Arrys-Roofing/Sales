Split HM-2 joist bracket

Two identical halves that slide in from each side and sit on the girder with a top flange, so nothing goes under the joist and it never gets jacked up.

ProjectHoneymoon Island State Park, Replace Structural Elements (61537C)
Based onHM-2 typical joist bracket, sheet S305, details 5, 9 and 10
Where4x12 joist bearing on a 4x16 girder ply
Material1/4″ 316 stainless plate, 3/4″ F593 thru bolts, per S305 notes

The idea

As drawn, HM-2 is one welded piece: a U that cradles the joist sitting on an upside-down U that straddles the girder, with a seat plate between them. To slide it in, the joist has to come up far enough to clear the side plates.

The split version drops the seat plate and cuts the bracket down the middle. Each half has a joist side plate, flanges down both faces of the girder, and a top flange that sits on the girder next to the joist. On each girder face the halves lap: Half A covers Half B on the back face and Half B covers Half A on the front, so each half locks the other in and the girder bolts pass through both layers. The halves slide toward each other along the girder until the side plates hit the joist, and the joist keeps bearing wood on wood on the girder. Both halves are the same part, with one turned around.

Install sequence

  1. Shore the joist at its current height.
  2. Remove the existing bracket.
  3. Slide each half in along the girder, top flange riding on the girder, until its side plate touches the joist.
  4. Run the thru bolts. The joist bolts pull both halves tight against the joist.
  5. Release the shore.

Questions for the engineer of record

  • Is it acceptable for the joist to bear directly on the girder with no seat plate between them?
  • Is a field-bolted two-piece bracket acceptable in place of the shop-welded HM-2?
  • Does it still meet the uplift and lateral loads HM-2 was designed for, with the girder bolts working through two lapped plates?
  • If the old bracket had a seat, is the ¼″ settlement when it comes out acceptable, or should the joist be shimmed?

Any change to HM-2 has to go to the engineer of record (Kever McKee Engineering) through the shop drawing or RFI process and be approved before anything is fabricated.

Dimensions are approximate, read from S305 details 9 and 10, and only one girder ply is shown. The gold and gray colors only tell the halves apart; both halves are 316 stainless.
